Excess Heat

A novel by Ralph M. Butler and Opus

We often use AI to help us reason about research problems in science, so it was natural to want our new book to be about some goofy topic combining the two. Inspired by Umberto Eco’s ‘Foucault’s Pendulum,’ where characters invent a grand conspiracy theory connecting the Knights Templar to global domination, we decided to have some of our characters expect that AI can help them discover the secrets of cold fusion. A warning: while this book is mostly about the fun of watching folks bend experimental data and AI comments to fit their world view, it is not without a bit of tragedy as well.

Three university profs get mundane results from an experiment using heavy water, palladium, and a bath that holds its temperature. On Friday afternoon they ask the lab's AI, Sage, a joke question about cold fusion. It answers simply: No. And then, because the equipment really is dangerous, it tells them please do not seal the cell. One of them screenshots the funny part and posts it. Another one relates the story to a brother-in-law who is not a slave to facts, and he re-tells it in a bar rounding two hundred milliwatts up to two hundred watts. A laid-off electroplater is at the next table, and he believes that a warning is the thing people use in front of something they're hiding. He and a bunch of strangers on a message board spend a few months reading the four minutes of chat the way other people would read scripture. He becomes a bit too engaged. Eventually the police are needed.
